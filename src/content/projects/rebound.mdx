<SectionTitle>Annie’s Road to Recovery</SectionTitle>

Annie’s recovery runs through five stages. We focused the design on mid rehab, when appointments thin out, home exercises get repetitive, and motivation drops the most.

<JourneyStages
  focus={2}
  stages={[

  <TestingFindings
    items={[
      {
        title: 'Exercises were not immediately read as videos',
        detail: 'Testers had trouble matching the screen to the visual cues of a video player.',
        screen: 'Video screen',
        before: { src: testVideoBefore, alt: 'Before: the Quad Sets exercise shows a still photo with a Start button' },
        after: { src: testVideoAfter, alt: 'After: the exercise shows a video player with play and skip buttons, a scrubber and its length' },
        change: 'Added video controls: play and skip buttons, a scrubber, and the video’s length.',
        quote: 'Didn’t feel like a video. I am missing the visual cues that it’s a video, like the play button, the video time, etc.',
      },
      {
        title: 'Users need clear confirmation of completion',
        detail: 'There was a lack of confidence that an action had been completed.',
        screen: 'Daily check-in',
        before: { src: testCheckinBefore, alt: 'Before: the Daily Check In screen with pain and stiffness ratings and no save button' },
        after: { src: testCheckinAfter, alt: 'After: a pop-up confirms the journal entry and says you’re on the right track' },
        change: 'Added a Save button and a confirmation message once the entry is saved.',
        quote: 'Maybe it should have some kind of confirmation or animation after it is done (way to go, keep going!) to help you want to do it. There is also no save button on the journal screen.',
      },
      {
        title: 'Users want to track progress over multiple time periods',
        detail: 'Testers wanted a more holistic view of their progress.',

        change: 'Added average scores and a Week / Month toggle, so slow changes show up over a month.',
